Project Title & Objective

Project Title: “Software Based Model Development for Design of Area-Specific Shelter for Thermal Comfort Maintenance”

Objective: To develop a software-based passive shelter design and thermal-comfort analysis tool. The tool allows students and engineers to enter area-specific climate conditions, shelter geometry, orientation, and construction materials, then execute a dynamic thermal analysis to evaluate indoor thermal comfort.

1. Dynamic Energy Balance & Lumped Thermal Capacitance Model

The building indoor air and internal envelope layers are modeled as a lumped dynamic thermal capacitance node governed by the first law of thermodynamics:

C_eff · (dT_in / dt) = Q_walls(t) + Q_roof(t) + Q_floor(t) + Q_win(t) + Q_vent(t) + Q_int

where C_eff = C_air + 0.30 · C_envelope (J/K) represents the participating thermal storage mass of the shelter, and dt = 900 seconds (15-minute discrete time steps). The simulation executes through 3 consecutive 24-hour cycles to eliminate initial condition bias and attain true periodic steady-state.

2. Sol-Air Temperature Formulation

Solar radiation absorbed on exterior opaque surfaces (walls and roof) is converted to an equivalent sol-air temperature ($T_{sol-air}$) according to ASHRAE fundamentals:

T_sol-air(t) = T_out(t) + [α · I_surf(t) / h_o] - [ε · ΔR / h_o]

where α is the surface solar absorptivity, I_surf(t) is the hourly incident solar radiation on the oriented surface (W/m²), h_o ≈ 22.7 W/m²·K is the exterior convective heat transfer coefficient, and ε·ΔR/h_o ≈ 4°C accounts for nocturnal long-wave radiation heat rejection to the sky.

3. Decrement Factor (f) and Thermal Time Lag (ϕ)

Thermal inertia performance is quantified through two dimensionless/temporal parameters:

Decrement Factor: f = (T_in,max - T_in,min) / (T_out,max - T_out,min)
Time Lag (ϕ): ϕ = Hour(T_in,max) - Hour(T_out,max) [hours]

High thermal mass envelopes (e.g., CSEB, Adobe, dense Stone) yield low decrement factors ($f < 0.45$) and significant time lags ($\phi \ge 4\text{ hours}$), shifting daytime heat peaks into the cooler evening.

4. Adaptive Thermal Comfort (ASHRAE Standard 55 & IMAC)

In naturally ventilated shelters, occupant thermal neutrality adapts to prevailing outdoor mean temperatures ($T_{out,mean}$):

Neutral Comfort Temperature: T_neutral = 17.8 + 0.31 · T_out,mean (°C)
80% Acceptability Comfort Limits: T_neutral ± 3.5 °C

Any hour where $T_{in}(t)$ falls within this range is counted as an "Estimated Comfort Hour".

Engineering Prototype Disclaimer

This application is an educational and design prototype developed for academic and conceptual passive solar analysis. It utilizes simplified, transparent first-principles heat transfer equations. It does not replace multi-million cell 3D Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) or finite-element transient thermal simulations (such as ANSYS Fluent or EnergyPlus). All calculated numerical outputs are explicitly designated as “Estimated / Model-based Results”.
